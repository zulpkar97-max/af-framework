## Core Thesis

Spherical Theory (Cognitive Sphere Hypothesis) proposes that each successfully solved problem and its AF path can be encapsulated as a cognitive module, namely a higher-order anchor [pending formalization]. If multiple cognitive modules can remain connected through low-curvature paths that are traceable, reusable, and calibratable, they may gradually form an extensible cognitive sphere [pending validation]. This sphere is not a one-time output, but a growing core of intelligence jointly constructed and maintained by the user and their intelligent agent over long-term collaboration [pending validation]. Its growth does not depend on the completion of a single task, but on successful paths being sedimented and reused in new problems.

## Relationship with AF

The AF Framework is located at the methodological implementation layer: it regulates operational constraints such as A/F compaction, bidirectional approximation, path curvature control, recursive stress testing, halt-on-gap, and human goal ownership in single-goal progression [see AF v0.1]. Spherical Theory is located at the upper purpose layer: it explains what kind of structure these cognitive modules, once calibrated through AF and sedimented over time, may form, and how such a structure may support continuous learning, cross-domain transfer, and the evolution of intelligence for the user and the intelligent agent [pending validation]. Therefore, AF v0.1 is not identical to Spherical Theory itself; AF v0.1 is the methodological implementation layer and evidence-generation entry point for Spherical Theory in concrete task progression.

## Conceptual Boundaries

This outline does not provide a complete argument, evidentiary chain, or mathematical formalization. Terms such as sphere, curvature, and module connection serve only as theoretical placeholders at this stage and do not constitute a quantifiable model [pending formalization]. This outline also does not claim that the theory has already realized AGI, or that it necessarily leads to any determinate form of intelligence. Its current function is only to publicly record Carl's core thesis on how problem-solving paths may be sedimented over time into a shared cognitive structure.
